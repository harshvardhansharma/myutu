Myutu — face reference

Hand-authored SVG translation of face_lab/face_lab.ino → shapes(). All thirteen expressions, resting state. This file is the design source of truth.

How to use this file

  1. Edit the SVG here first. Every shape carries a comment naming the exact C++ call it came from, with the arguments already resolved.
  2. Port the change into face_lab/face_lab.ino → shapes(), keeping the primitive the comment names.
  3. Then port the same change into myutu_pet/myutu_pet.ino, which carries its own copy of the drawing code.

Coordinates. The sketch draws into a 240×172 band sprite pushed to y = SPR_Y = 28, so every y inside shapes() is sprite-local. The SVGs below are in panel coordinates, so every y in a comment has had SPR_Y added back. EYE_CY and MOUTH_Y are already declared with − SPR_Y baked in, so they land back on 108 and 170; bare literals such as (62 - SPR_Y) in nervous likewise resolve to panel 62. Anything written as ey + 47 is sprite-local arithmetic and becomes 108 + 47 here.

Every face is drawn at rest: fOpen = 1.0 (eyes fully open), fSacX = fSacY = 0 (gaze centred), per-face oscillations at t = 0. Blink and saccade apply to every face and are not shown. Where t = 0 is not the visual centre of an oscillation the card says so.

This is a reference sheet — nothing here animates. Motion is described in words under each card.

Palette — five concentric passes

draw() calls shapes(e, t, li) for li = 0..4. Each pass grows every shape by PAL_GROW[li] and paints it in that layer's colour, widest and dimmest first. shapes() receives the layer index, not a colour, so a single element can opt into a different palette and still get the same bloom.

BG is #000000 — used both as the panel ground and as a punch-out colour. Hex values are the C565 macro's inputs; the panel quantises them to RGB565.

Only two elements leave PAL_WHITE: the angry spark (PAL_RED) and the crying tear streams (PAL_BLUE).

Geometry below is the core pass, li = 4, g = 0. That is the pass to edit; the four halos are the same shapes grown by g.

neutral case NEUTRAL

idle

Nothing face-specific moves. Only the global blink (fOpen collapses the eye height) and the saccade (fSacX ±8, fSacY ±3, darting in 90 ms and holding).

surprised case SURPRISED

one tap

A held breath: the eye radius pulses 38 ±1.5 px on a 420 ms sine — t = 0 is the centre of that swing. Brows are gated on wide (fOpen > 0.35), so they vanish for the middle of every blink.

happy case HAPPY

two quick taps

Deliberately still — the calmest face in the set. No per-face oscillation at all; only the global blink and saccade, and fOpen squashes the 64 px eye height on the blink. The brows and the counterphase brow wiggle that used to live here were both removed.

dizzy case DIZZY

shake

Both X's orbit on a 170 ms cycle — left by (5·sin, 4·cos), right by the negative, so they counter-rotate. The mouth wave scrolls, its phase driven straight off t. Drawn off-centre: at t = 0 the cosine term is at its extreme, so the two X's are 8 px apart vertically rather than level.

sleepy case SLEEPY

idle 20 s

Losing the fight: droop swings 0.62 → 1.00 on a 2.4 s sine, so the lid bar eats more or less of the eye and it catches itself again. Drawn at droop = 0.81, the exact centre of the swing.

sleeping case SLEEPING

idle 45 s

Breathes on a 1.4 s sine: lids and mouth ride ±4 px and the mouth radius swells 2 → 6. Three Z's loop on a 2.6 s cycle, each rising 50 px, drifting 20 px right and growing from text size 1 to 3.

curious case CURIOUS

tilt and hold

Looks left, then right, on a 1.8 s sine: the gaze sweeps look = ±12 px, the head tilt trades ±8 px between the two eyes, and the far eye shrinks to 0.74× at the extremes. The raised brow only appears once |ph| > 0.35. Drawn at mid-sweep, where by design there is no asymmetry left at all except the right eye's tighter corner radius — so this card is the least representative of the thirteen.

scared case SCARED

free fall

Trembles: the two eyes shift ±2 px in opposition on a fast 120 ms sine, so they pinch together and apart. The mouth wave scrolls at 1.5× the dizzy rate.

love case LOVE

three quick taps

Three hearts loop up the right side on a 2.6 s cycle, staggered a third apart — the same timing, rise and growth as the sleeping Z's. Each climbs 54 px, drifts 16 px right and grows from size 5 to 12, then snaps back. t = 0 is a snapshot of the loop, not a centre: it catches the newest heart exactly at its spawn point.

angry case ANGRY

shake again within 4 s

The single red spark pulses its radius 11 ±4 px on a 160 ms sine — faster and harder than anything else on the face, so the anger reads as having its own pulse. Nothing else moves. The brows are gated on wide, so they drop out mid-blink and the eyes briefly lose the scowl.

eating case EATING

trigger TBD — see state machine

The offset mouth chews on a fast 190 ms cycle: ch = |sin(t/190)| drives its height 8 → 22 px and lifts its top edge 11 px, so it opens both upward and downward around y ≈ 170. Using |sin| rather than sin doubles the rate and makes the chew uneven. Drawn at ch = 0, the closed extreme — the mouth is never smaller than this.

nervous case NERVOUS

trigger TBD — see state machine

The sweat drop slides ±5 px vertically on a slow 700 ms sine and never quite falls; that slide is the whole expression. The mouth wave scrolls at 0.8× the dizzy rate, slower than scared. The eyes are static, the right one permanently 8% smaller.

crying case CRYING

trigger TBD — see state machine

The two streams do not fall — they ripple in place. wob shifts each segment ±3 px sideways on a sine of yy·0.16 + t/150, so a wave with a 39 px wavelength travels up the column once every ~940 ms and the flow reads as moving. The eyes are static and there is no mouth. t = 0 is an arbitrary phase of that ripple, not a centre — the column is never straight.

Translated from face_lab/face_lab.ino → shapes(), thirteen faces, core pass (li = 4, g = 0). Arithmetic is lifted exactly, including the truncating (int) casts and integer divisions. Known approximations:

  • The bloom. feMorphology dilate grows with a square structuring element, while the C++ grows circles into circles. On the widest pass (g = 7) a large disc bulges about 4 px at the diagonals; on g = 1 and g = 3 it is invisible. The four radii, four colours and the merge order are exact.
  • Punch-outs. The black BG fills (the arcUpper/arcLower cut circles, the sleepy lid bar) are merged on top of the halo, so a concave edge gets no outward bloom here. In the C++ the punch is itself grown and displaced on every pass, so the real panel blooms a few pixels past the arch's inner edge.
  • thickLine. Now a filled quad with round caps, so a stroked <line> with stroke-linecap="round" is a faithful translation — not the approximation it was against the old stacked-line version. The only residue: the C++ caps have integer radius t/2 (4 for t = 9) where SVG uses the exact half-width (4.5).
  • mouthWave. Drawn as one <polyline> with round joins and caps, which is geometrically identical to the eight consecutive thickLine calls it replaces.
  • The sleeping Z's. A system monospace face stands in for the Adafruit GFX 5×7 bitmap. The GFX cursor is the cell's top-left, so the baseline is estimated at +7 px per text size unit.
  • Rasterisation. The panel draws integer, aliased pixels into a 16-bit sprite and quantises every colour to RGB565; the SVG is antialiased and full 24-bit.

Everything else — every coordinate, radius, thickness and polygon vertex — is the resolved value of the C++ call named in the comment beside it, with SPR_Y = 28 added back to convert sprite-local y into panel y.
